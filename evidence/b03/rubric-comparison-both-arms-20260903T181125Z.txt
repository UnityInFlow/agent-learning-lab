=== AN UNREGISTERED FINDING, REPORTED AS EXPLORATORY ===

`test-quality` is null on 4 of 10 treatment runs and 0 of 10 control runs. Every null carries
the same reason from the scorer: "nothing to grade".

IT IS NOT A SCORER ARTIFACT. The nulls track exactly the runs that changed no test file, and
that was checked twice — once against `result.changedFiles` on the run record, once against
`git status` in the kept worktree:

  a472f54c  369b9d03  514df7f7  71d77855   2 files changed, no test file    TREATMENT
  all ten control runs                     3 files changed, test file included

  Fisher exact two-sided, 4/10 vs 0/10:  p = 0.087

The scorer reported the absence correctly. `null` is the rubric doing its job — P7, "unknown
data remains unknown" — and the four sheets are evidence for that, not against it.

WHAT MAKES THIS UNCOMFORTABLE. The treatment arm is the arm carrying R2: "Run the module's
verification command before reporting the work complete." The arm told to verify wrote tests
LESS often — 6 of 10 against 10 of 10 — and the direction is the opposite of what anyone
would write down in advance.

WHY IT IS NOT A RESULT OF THIS EXPERIMENT. "Wrote a test file at all" was never registered
as an outcome. R2's registered outcomes are `toolCalls` and duration, both null. This number
was found by looking at the data after the fact, p = 0.087 does not clear 0.05, and a
post-hoc outcome selected because it looked interesting is exactly the failure mode this
project has voided results over. It is recorded here, in full, as a hypothesis that has not
been tested — never as a finding of E-003.

A MECHANISM IT WOULD BE CHEAP TO TEST. "Run the verification command" may redirect effort
from WRITING checks to RUNNING existing ones: the suite already passes, so an agent that
takes the rule seriously runs `./mvnw test`, sees green, and reports done. If that is right,
the rule does not reduce diligence — it satisfies diligence with the cheaper of two actions,
and it does so because the task's acceptance ("Existing tests pass", task.md:25) does not
require a new one. That is a specific, falsifiable claim and it is the registered
prediction for the follow-up rather than a conclusion here.
