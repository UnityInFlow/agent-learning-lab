## The five things worth carrying out of stop 21

1. **Author decision 9's headroom fix does not transfer across runtimes.** BE-004 was built because
   BE-003 had 50 of 100 rubric points at zero variance on `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`. On
   `gpt-5.6-sol`, **both tasks' controls sit at the ceiling of the registered dimension alike** — 5 of
   5 and 5 of 5 at the maximum of a 0–2 scale. A headroom fix is a property of a (task, model) pair,
   not of a task. **Anything that changes the model must re-measure headroom before registering an
   outcome**, and that measurement costs 18 seconds of codex per control run, which this batch did not
   spend and should have.

2. **Registering an MDE as *undefined* is honest and is not sufficient.** Both experiments registered
   *"undefined before the batch; the concurrent control is its first measurement"* — which was true,
   was the only available statement, and still let a batch run whose outcome could not move upward.
   The missing step is not a number; it is **a control-arm rubric census before the outcome is
   chosen**. That is the cheapest instrument this track is still missing and it is now in
   `author_notes`.

3. **Delivery, uptake and effect are three things, and this stop separated all three for the first
   time.** Stop 20 separated the first two: 20 of 20 delivered, 3 of 20 consulted. Stop 21 adds the
   third: **10 of 10 delivered, 10 of 10 consulted, 0 of 10 effect the instrument could see.** A
   corpus can arrive, be read by every run, and still move nothing — and on this evidence the reason
   was the ceiling rather than the corpus, which is why no keep/remove decision was taken on it.
   **§4 step 10's "a rule with no measured effect is removed" does not apply to an outcome with no
   headroom**, and conflating the two would have deleted the mechanism the co-variates point at.

4. **DF1's rule is the best one-line statement of P5 this track has produced, and it is `n = 1`.** In
   one run, from one directory, `.ai/knowledge/router.sh` wrote its log and `.ai/hooks/policy-gate.sh`
   did not. Two shell scripts, one overlay, one runtime. The difference is **who invokes it**:

   > **What ports is what the model can call. What does not port is what the runtime must call.**

   That is a rule a reader can apply to a file they have not seen, which `8 of 11` is not. It is
   stated as true of that run and **not** as a property (§5).

5. **A negative observation is worth exactly as much as the explanations it excludes.** DF-P2 is *"no
   hook log appeared"*. It is only evidence because six alternatives were closed by command: not
   installed (`git ls-tree` on the setup commit), installed non-executable (`100755` — the mode bit no
   hash sees, stop 20's precedent), deleted mid-run (`ls -l` the worktree), broken and dying silently
   (the gate invoked **from the run's own worktree** afterwards → exit 2 and a `deny` line), an empty
   trigger population (3 changed Kotlin files, all `Edit`/`Write` targets), and — the one a validator
   should ask hardest — **a detector never shown to fire**, closed by `verify-b10-df-guards.sh` case L,
